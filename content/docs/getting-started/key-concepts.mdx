AccountWise is built on a small set of ideas. Understanding these makes every
module easier to use.

## Chart of Accounts

The **Chart of Accounts** is the backbone of the system. It is organized into
five primary account types:

| Account Type | What it represents |
| --- | --- |
| Assets | What the business owns |
| Liabilities | What the business owes |
| Equity | The owner's stake in the business |
| Revenue | Income the business earns |
| Expense | Costs the business incurs |

Every transaction eventually posts to one or more of these accounts. See
[Chart of Accounts](/docs/accounting/chart-of-accounts) for details.

## Journal entries

A **journal entry** is the record of debits and credits behind a transaction.
AccountWise creates these automatically when you record sales and purchases, so
your books stay balanced without manual work. See [Journal
Entries](/docs/accounting/journal-entries).

## Documents

Most of your work in AccountWise happens through **documents** — invoices,
sales orders, bills, purchase orders, receipts, and so on. Documents share
common behaviors:

- Many can be **cloned** to save time.
- Listing pages can be **filtered** (for example, by customer or vendor).
- Every document shows its **History** and **GL Impact** at the bottom.

<Callout type="info">
  "GL Impact" means General Ledger impact — the journal entry the document
  created. You can review it on any document to see exactly how it affected your
  accounts.
</Callout>

## The sales and purchase cycles

Business activity flows through two mirrored cycles:

- The **sales cycle** starts with a customer and ends with money received.
- The **purchase cycle** starts with a vendor and ends with money paid.

See [The AccountWise Workflow](/docs/getting-started/the-accountwise-workflow)
for the full picture.
